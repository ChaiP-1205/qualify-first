## Why it exists

At this stage, outreach is manual. Every lead from a list, an event or a referral needs research before you can write a believable first email, and much of that time goes to leads that were never going to be a fit.

Qualify First is the quick first look for that stage. It tells you whether a lead is worth a message, what matters about them and who to reach, and only then drafts a short first email. Your research time goes to the leads that deserve it. Paid enrichment can come later, once you know which leads are worth the credits.

It's just as useful when a lead comes to you, from a website form or a reply. You get the same first look and talking points before you answer or get on a discovery call, so you never go in blank.

## What you get

- **A clear verdict.** Every company gets one of four answers: **Strong fit**, **Possible fit**, **Not a fit** or **Can't tell yet**. The reasons sit right below it, so you can see why at a glance.
- **Switch the ICP, switch the verdict.** Fit is checked against the seller's ideal customer profile (ICP), so the same company can be a Strong fit for one seller and Not a fit for another. "See the ICP" shows what each is looking for.
- **A quick first look.** What they do, who they sell to, size, funding and recent news in one place, each fact linked to its source.
- **Talking points.** A couple of facts worth raising in the email, on a discovery call or in a client meeting.
- **Your own context.** Add a note like "met at a conference" and it shapes the email.
- **Who to contact.** The roles to reach first for that kind of company, even if you don't know the industry.
- **A short first email.** Written from real facts about the company, and only for leads worth contacting.
- **No made-up facts.** Anything it can't source is left out and listed under "What we couldn't confirm". When the evidence is thin it says "Can't tell yet" instead of guessing.
- **Existing customers spotted.** If a company already uses the seller's product, it tells you to route them to their account manager, not sales.
